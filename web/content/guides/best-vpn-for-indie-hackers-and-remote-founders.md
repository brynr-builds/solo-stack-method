You're building from a laptop in a café, a co-living space, or a hotel room in another
country. The Wi-Fi is free, the connection is fine, and the security is a joke. A VPN is
not a luxury for a remote builder — it's the one line of defense between your code, your
accounts, and whoever else is on that network.

This is the short version: there are two VPNs that make sense for a solo founder or indie
hacker in 2026, and the choice comes down to one question — **do you need one device or
unlimited?**

## The 30-second answer

- **One device, want the best security and speed?** → **NordVPN.** Strongest encryption
  defaults, fastest servers in most regions, and the cleanest app. Priced per device.
- **Multiple devices, want the best value?** → **Surfshark.** Unlimited devices on one
  subscription, same core security, lower price. The practical pick if you have a laptop,
  a phone, a tablet, and a partner's laptop all on the same account.

There is no "best VPN" in the abstract. There's a best one for your device count and your
budget. Below is how we'd actually choose.

## NordVPN — best for security-first, single-device users

**Best for:** the builder who wants the strongest defaults and doesn't want to think about
it. One laptop, one phone, done.

- **Pros:** strongest encryption defaults (AES-256, double VPN option); fastest server
  network in most benchmarks; clean, fast app on macOS, Windows, iOS, Android; kill switch
  that actually works; 30-day money-back guarantee.
- **Cons:** priced per device (you pay more if you want 5+ devices on one account); the
  "best value" pricing only kicks in on the 2-year plan, which is a real commitment.
- **The catch we'd flag:** if you're on a tight budget and have more than two devices, the
  per-device pricing adds up fast. That's where Surfshark wins.

## Surfshark — best for unlimited devices on one bill

**Best for:** the builder who has a laptop, a phone, a tablet, a work machine, and maybe a
partner's device — and wants them all covered on one subscription.

- **Pros:** unlimited devices on a single subscription (this is the killer feature); same
  core security as NordVPN (AES-256, kill switch, no-logs policy); lower price per year;
  30-day money-back guarantee; clean app.
- **Cons:** slightly slower than NordVPN in some regions (not a problem for most use
  cases); the "unlimited devices" claim is real but the app can feel a little less
  polished than Nord's on macOS.
- **The catch we'd flag:** if you're in a region where Surfshark's server network is
  thinner, you may notice a speed difference. Test the free trial in your actual location
  before committing to a 2-year plan.

## The honest decision tree

- One laptop + one phone, want the best security, don't want to think → **NordVPN.**
- Multiple devices, want the best value, same core security → **Surfshark.**
- You're in a country with heavy internet restrictions → **NordVPN** (stronger
  obfuscation, more reliable in restricted networks).
- You're on a tight budget and have 3+ devices → **Surfshark.**

## One thing most VPN guides won't tell you

A VPN protects you from the network you're on. It does **not** protect you from a
compromised password, a phishing link, or a malicious extension in your browser. If you're
building with AI tools and you have API keys, repo tokens, and payment info in your
browser, a VPN is necessary but not sufficient. Use a password manager, enable 2FA on
everything, and keep your API keys in environment variables — not in code, not in a
browser bookmark. (This is the same governance principle the [Solo Stack Method](/) is
built on: the tool is a detail; the habit is the asset.)
